Please consider the manuscript "Duality and Complementarity of Vector and Interpolation-Inspired Barrier Certificates for Safety Verification: Toward Reduced Conservatism and Complexity" for publication in Systems & Control Letters.

The paper studies when vector and interpolation-inspired barrier certificates are structurally equivalent and when their different propagation restrictions become complementary. We establish a degree-preserving bidirectional correspondence for a robust anchored globally scaled path-matrix subclass, including exact transport of SOS witnesses. Beyond that common subclass, we establish opposite fixed-degree obstructions: cyclic vector coupling yields affine certificates where finite implication IBCs require degree two, while an author-constructed nonlinear example admits an affine implication IBC even though every affine constant-comparison VBC is excluded on the fixed verification domain. These results motivate changing certificate structure before automatically increasing polynomial degree and quantify the associated dense polynomial/SOS sizing implications without claiming universal runtime dominance.

The computational artifact separates numerical candidate generation from exact-rational verification. The historical SOS claims were re-audited, the original S2 domain error was corrected explicitly, external/adapted examples are labeled by provenance, and analytical nonexistence results are kept separate from solver failures.
